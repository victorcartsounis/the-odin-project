Carbonara Recipe

Carbonara Dish

Carbonara is a classic Italian pasta dish known for its rich, silky texture and bold, savory flavor. Traditionally made with spaghetti, it’s tossed with crispy guanciale (cured pork cheek), eggs, Pecorino Romano cheese, and freshly ground black pepper. The magic of carbonara lies in its technique: the residual heat of the pasta gently cooks the eggs into a creamy sauce, without the need for any cream, coating each strand in a luscious, velvety finish.

The flavor is a perfect balance of salty, umami depth from the guanciale and cheese, lifted by the sharpness of black pepper. When done well, carbonara feels indulgent yet simple, highlighting how a handful of high-quality ingredients and precise timing can come together to create something deeply satisfying and elegant.

Ingredients

  • 200g spaghetti
  • 100g guanciale (or pancetta)
  • 2 large eggs
  • 50g Pecorino Romano cheese, grated
  • Freshly ground black pepper
  • Salt (for pasta water)

Instructions

  1. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Add the spaghetti and cook until al dente, according to package instructions. Reserve about 1 cup of pasta water, then drain the pasta.
  2. While the pasta cooks, cut the guanciale into small strips. In a large skillet over medium heat, cook the guanciale until it’s crispy and golden brown. Remove from heat and set aside.
  3. In a bowl, whisk together the eggs, grated Pecorino Romano cheese, and a generous amount of freshly ground black pepper until well combined.
  4. Add the hot, drained spaghetti to the skillet with the guanciale (off the heat). Quickly pour the egg and cheese mixture over the pasta, tossing vigorously to combine. The residual heat from the pasta will cook the eggs and create a creamy sauce. If the sauce is too thick, add reserved pasta water a little at a time until you reach your desired consistency.
  5. Serve immediately, garnished with extra Pecorino Romano and black pepper if desired.
